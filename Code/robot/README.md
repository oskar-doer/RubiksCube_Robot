# Rubik's Cube Robot Control

## Programs

- `esp32_cube_controller`: reads the QiYi cube via Bluetooth, calculates a
  solution with min2phase, displays the state on the LCD, and sends the move
  sequence to the F6 via UART.
- `f6_motion_controller`: receives the move sequence, generates STEP/DIR signals
  for the six TMC2209 drivers, and reports progress and measured movement time.

The F6 executes directly consecutive moves of opposite faces in parallel: U
with D, R with L, and F with B. The remaining sequence stays unchanged.

The motor assignment, direction of rotation, and steps per 90 degrees have been
verified. Motor movement is enabled in the F6 program with
`MOTION_CONFIGURATION_CONFIRMED=true`.

The complete connections for the power supply, LCD, controllers, motor drivers,
and motors are documented in [WIRING.md](WIRING.md).

## Operation

After a valid cube connection, the LCD displays `Cube verbunden` ("Cube
connected"). Briefly
pressing the ESP's BOOT button (GPIO0) solves a scrambled cube. If the Bluetooth
cube is already solved, the same button starts a random 20-move scramble.
Pressing it again during the motor run aborts the movement. The serial command
`s` starts the same state-dependent action; `x` aborts it.
As soon as the F6 reports `START`, the LCD shows the elapsed time and move
progress. After `DONE`, the ESP confirms success exclusively based on the state
subsequently reported by the Bluetooth cube. `x` aborts an ongoing motor
sequence.

After a confirmed solution, the LCD displays the motor time alone and the move
count. The USB monitor additionally shows the solver, motor, and total times.
The solver keeps the first result as a fallback and continues searching for a
total of three seconds for the fastest sequence with no more than 30 moves.
Directly consecutive moves of opposite faces count together as one time step
that can be executed in parallel.

The BOOT button may only be pressed after power-up or reset. If it is held
during a reset, the ESP starts in download mode. The RST button remains the
reset button.

## Measured Motion Values

- Reference measurement at 16 microsteps: 400 pulses produce approximately 45 degrees.
- Reference measurement at 16 microsteps: 800 pulses therefore produce 90 degrees.
- In the production profile with 2 microsteps, 100 pulses therefore correspond
  to a 90-degree turn.
- `+`, or DIR HIGH, turns counterclockwise when looking at the motor from the
  front. This means viewing it from the shaft/adapter side.
- Because the shaft/adapter side faces the cube, DIR HIGH corresponds to a
  clockwise move when looking at the respective colored cube face from the outside.

## Setting Speed and Motor Current

The clearly visible settings are at the beginning of
`f6_motion_controller.ino` in the `EINFACHE BEWEGUNGSEINSTELLUNGEN` ("simple
motion settings") block:

- `STEPS_PER_90_DEGREES = 100`: pulses for 90 degrees at 2 microsteps
- `START_SPEED_DEG_PER_SEC = 500`: speed at the beginning/end of a turn
- `MAX_SPEED_DEG_PER_SEC = 2400`: peak speed for 90-degree moves
- `MAX_SPEED_180_DEG_PER_SEC = 2600`: peak speed for 180-degree moves
- `ACCELERATION_STEPS = 8`: ramp for 90-degree moves
- `ACCELERATION_180_STEPS = 14`: longer ramp for 180-degree moves
- `MOTOR_RMS_CURRENT_MA = 1700`: running current per motor; 1700 corresponds to 1.7 A RMS
- `MOTOR_HOLD_MULTIPLIER = 0.35`: holding current for stationary motors
- `DRIVER_MICROSTEPS = 2`: microsteps configured via UART with interpolation
- `USE_SPREADCYCLE = true`: more torque during fast movements

The STEP pulses are generated by Timer1 at absolute times. As a result, serial
responses and emergency-stop polling do not change the motor speed. Opposite
faces continue to move simultaneously; individual speeds for each motor are
intentionally not used.

At startup, the F6 configures all six TMC2209 drivers via UART and checks
`test_connection = 0` and `VERSION = 0x21` for each one. If any driver does not
respond, all motor commands remain disabled and the ESP receives `DRIVER_UART`.
